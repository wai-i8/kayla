# Guide media and illustration policy

Last updated: **28 August 2026**.

## Local media policy

- The KAYLA guide does **not** bundle or render photographs of real people.
- Every local static guide picture is an original AI-generated hand-painted illustration created for KAYLA.
- Official demonstrations may still be offered as plain external links. Their photographs, thumbnails and videos remain on the publisher's website and are not copied, embedded or rehosted by KAYLA.
- A guide illustration is a memory aid only. Medical or safety wording must continue to cite and follow the current NHS, UK Government, Resuscitation Council UK, UNICEF UK or other named primary source.

## Illustration style contract

Use this visual system for every future guide illustration:

- Soft feminine storybook watercolour／gouache style with delicate warm-brown linework.
- Blush pink, peach, cream and warm brown palette, with only very pale sage accents.
- Off-white background and rounded vertical panels or rounded scene framing.
- Keep the same visual identity: a warm-brown-haired East Asian mother／caregiver and a baby girl with short brown hair, usually in a pale-pink sleepsuit.
- Use gentle leaves, hearts or sparkles sparingly; the care action must remain the focal point.
- Use newborn proportions and anatomically plausible hands, limbs and facial features. Show safe head and neck support whenever the baby is held.
- Make the action understandable at phone size. Use pose, gaze and restrained motion marks instead of decorative scenes that hide the instruction.
- Do not include photorealism, copied official artwork, watermarks, logos or embedded instructional text.
- Default production size is **1448 × 1086 (4:3)**. Export web JPEG at roughly 85–88 quality and aim to stay below 500 KB where practical.

The current style reference is `public/guide-media/illustration-feeding-cues-v2.jpg`, together with the other `illustration-*.jpg` files in that directory.

## Medical illustration checks

Before adding or replacing an instructional image:

1. Recheck the underlying action against the current primary guidance.
2. Describe exactly what the image demonstrates in its alt text and caption.
3. Remove or redraw ambiguous details instead of presenting them as proof of a diagnosis or a correct technique.
4. For behaviour cues, remind readers to consider several signs and the baby's normal pattern; one isolated action may have more than one meaning.
5. Keep emergency actions and thresholds in text. Do not rely on an illustration alone.

## Official link-only demonstrations

These resources stay on their publishers' websites. KAYLA only displays a text link and does not use their photographs or poster frames:

- [NHS — Breastfeeding: positioning and attachment](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/breastfeeding-and-bottle-feeding/breastfeeding/positioning-and-attachment/)
- [NHS — How to change your baby's nappy](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/how-to-change-your-babys-nappy/)
- [NHS — Bathing your baby](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/baby-basics/caring-for-your-baby/bathing-your-baby/)
- [Resuscitation Council UK — Baby and child CPR](https://www.resus.org.uk/baby-cpr)

In a real emergency, call **999**, put the phone on loudspeaker and follow the call handler's instructions. Online media are refreshers, not a substitute for hands-on first-aid training.
